3. Historical data, not predictions

Seasonal tendencies are averages of how markets behaved in past years. They have real limits:

  • Any single year can, and often does, behave differently from the average.
  • A few extreme years can shape an average more than they should.
  • Market structure changes over time, so a pattern from decades ago may no longer hold.
  • Futures data is read from published charts, and forex pairs are synthesised from two currency futures. Both steps can introduce errors.

4. Backtests and track record

Past performance does not predict future results. Backtests on the Upload tab and figures on the Track Record page are hypothetical. They do not include spreads, commissions, swaps, slippage or the timing of real orders.

Track-record results marked backfilled are graded using seasonal data that already contains those years, so they are likely to look better than live results would have. Only results marked live-recorded reflect signals as they were shown at the time.

5. AI analysis

The Analysis tab sends data to a third-party AI model you choose (Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini, or a local Ollama model), and shows what it generates. AI output can be wrong: it may misread data, be incomplete, sound more confident than it should, or contradict the other tabs.

A LONG, SHORT, NEUTRAL or WAIT verdict is model output, not a trading signal and not advice. Verify it independently before relying on it.
